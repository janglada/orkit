    Glossary¶

    Plain-language meanings of the terms used on this site, in alphabetical order.

    Bound
    A limit that the solver has proven no plan can beat. If you minimize and the bound is 1073, no plan scores below 1073. See Diagnostics.
    Buffer (booking)
    Time after an appointment during which the resource stays blocked, for example to clean a room. The appointment itself does not get longer.
    Builder
    The object you describe your problem with. You get one from Optimize, for example Optimize.jobShop().
    Capacity
    How much something can hold or do: the weight a van carries, the number of jobs a machine runs at once.
    Constraint
    A rule that a plan must follow. See also hard and soft.
    CP-SAT
    The OR-Tools engine behind every domain except routing. It can prove that a plan is the best possible.
    Deadline (job-shop)
    A time by which a step must end. A plan that misses it is not allowed. Compare due date.
    Demand (routing)
    How much of a vehicle's capacity a stop uses.
    Depot (routing)
    The place where vehicles start and end their routes.
    Domain
    One kind of problem that orkit can solve, such as job-shop scheduling or vehicle routing. There are seven.
    Downtime (job-shop)
    A period when a machine cannot work, such as maintenance.
    Due date (job-shop)
    A time by which a step should end. Being late is allowed but counts against the plan. Compare deadline.
    Feasible
    A plan that follows all the hard rules. Also a status: FEASIBLE means "a valid plan, not proven to be the best".
    Gap
    How far a plan might be from the best possible, as a fraction. A gap of 0.05 means the best possible plan is at most 5% better. See Diagnostics.
    Goal (also objective)
    What "best" means for your problem: shortest, cheapest, most valuable.
    Guillotine cut (cutting)
    A straight cut that goes all the way across a sheet, from one edge to the other. Many saws can only cut this way.
    Hard rule
    A rule that can never be broken. If the hard rules clash, there is no plan.
    Haversine distance (routing)
    The straight-line distance between two points on the globe, "as the crow flies". Shorter than the distance by road.
    Heuristic
    A method that finds good answers quickly without proving that they are the best. Routing uses heuristics.
    Horizon
    The period that a plan covers, for example "seven days starting Monday 6 July".
    Id
    The name you give to a thing in your model, such as "Van-1". Ids must be unique within their kind.
    Infeasible
    No plan can follow all the hard rules at once.
    Kerf (cutting)
    The material that the saw blade turns into dust at every cut.
    Makespan (job-shop)
    The time at which the last step of the whole schedule ends. A short makespan means everything is finished early.
    Metaheuristic (routing)
    A method for improving routes that can escape when small changes no longer help.
    Model
    Your description of the problem: the things, their details and the rules.
    Objective
    The score of a plan for the chosen goal. objective() returns it.
    Operation (job-shop)
    One step of a job: a machine and a duration.
    Optimal
    The best possible plan, with proof that none is better.
    Penalty
    A number added to the score when something undesirable happens, such as skipping a stop. A larger penalty makes the solver try harder to avoid it.
    Precedence (job-shop)
    A rule that one thing must finish before another starts.
    Release time (job-shop)
    The earliest time a step may start, for example when the material arrives.
    Resource (booking)
    Anything an appointment needs and that can be busy: a person, a room, a machine.
    Roster (scheduling)
    The finished plan of who works which shift.
    Setup time (job-shop)
    Time needed to prepare a machine between two different kinds of work, such as cleaning between two paint colors.
    Soft rule
    A rule that may be broken at a price. The solver tries to follow it but will break it when the alternative is worse.
    Solver
    The engine that searches for the best plan. In orkit you get one with solver().
    Tardiness (job-shop)
    How late something is compared with its due date. Zero when it is on time.
    Time window
    A period during which something is allowed, such as a shop that accepts deliveries from 9:00 to 11:00.
    Warm start
    Starting a search from a plan you already have, instead of from nothing.
    Weight (of a goal)
    A number that says how much a goal counts compared with the others.
